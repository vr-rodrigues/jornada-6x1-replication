\section{Data sources}\label{app:data}

All data are from publicly available sources. PNAD Cont\'{i}nua (IBGE/SIDRA), tables 4093, 4097, 6374, and 6413 (quarterly, 2023--2025), provides the informality rate, position in occupation, average hours, firm-size distribution, and the CES formal weight $\omega=0.622$ ($1$ minus the narrow PNAD informality rate averaged over the four quarters of 2025). RAIS/CAGED (Ministry of Labor) provides annual administrative records of formal employment, used for firm-size distribution and formal/informal employment shares. The Penn World Table 10.01 provides the labor share for Brazil ($\text{labsh}=0.578$), adjusted via \citet{Gollin2002} to $\alpha=0.35$. DIEESE (2024) provides the hours distribution across the formal workforce: $\theta=(0.085, 0.269, 0.646)$ for the $\{36,40,44\}$ hours bins. An auditing exercise in the replication package verifies that the non-targeted moments in the main text are not algebraic consequences of the calibration.

\section{What partial equilibrium omits}\label{app:pe_omits}

The static partial-equilibrium framework excludes seven general-equilibrium channels. Capital accumulation responds to the lower marginal product of capital after the effective labor contraction. Wages and prices adjust in general equilibrium, partially restoring formal labor demand. Labor force participation may rise with leisure. Monetary and fiscal authorities may accommodate. Workers may reallocate across sectors. The formalization decision may move in either direction. Work-sharing is shut down by the fixed-$N$ assumption. All seven channels except the last have a negative expected sign on $A_{\text{req}}$.

The capital-adjustment bound in Equation~\eqref{eq:pe_bound} of the main paper uses a textbook user-cost elasticity $\varepsilon_K\in[0.2,0.4]$ \citep{Caballero1999} and the baseline capital share $\alpha=0.35$. At these elasticities over a $3$--$5$ year horizon, the effective $A_{\text{req}}$ under the conservative specification falls from $8.18\%$ to $6.4\%$--$7.2\%$, and under the preferred specification to $4.8\%$--$5.6\%$. The remaining six channels are individually small and of mixed sign. The headline numbers should be read as short-run upper bounds on the required TFP gain. The $1$--$2$ year model horizon is not the same as the 30-year PWT series used to anchor $A_{\text{req}}$. The anchor is best read as the cumulative TFP gain required over a $3$--$10$ year phase-in horizon.

\section{Calibration details}\label{app:calibration_details}

\subsection{Hours-output elasticity and efficiency shape}

The Pencavel evidence documents declining marginal product of hours at long workweeks for a specific industrial setting. \citet{CollewetSauermann2017} find analogous long-hour productivity losses in a Dutch call-center panel. \citet{BickFuchsSchundelnLagakos2018} provide cross-country evidence consistent with declining marginal product of hours, though their primary focus is the relationship between hours and income. Neither paper identifies a precise peak location or an efficiency response below a peak. The choice to place the peak at $h^*=40$ and to compare a preferred flat-below specification (no penalty for shorter hours) with a conservative symmetric specification (equal curvature either side) is a modeling choice loosely informed by this evidence rather than a direct estimate from it. Section~\ref{app:asymmetric} reports four alternative shapes.

\subsection{Informal efficiency and adjustment costs}

The informal efficiency $\eta_I=0.40$ is set at the midpoint of the informal-to-formal wage ratio range ($1/3$ to $1/2$) reported by \citet{LaPortaShleifer2014}, complemented by the Mexican estimates in \citet{BussoFazioLevy2012}. Under competitive wage-taking the CES first-order conditions identify $\eta_I$ through the wage (marginal-product) ratio rather than through value-added per employee, which conflates productivity differences with capital-per-worker gaps between formal and informal firms. The adjustment costs $\gamma_F^S=0.12$ and $\gamma_F^L=0.03$ reflect the effective marginal compliance cost of CLT/FGTS obligations for small and large firms. Sensitivity over $\gamma_F^S\in[0.08,0.16]$ and $\gamma_F^L\in[0.01,0.05]$ shifts $A_{\text{req}}$ by less than $\pm 0.2$ pp. The convex informality cost $\pi_m$ is calibrated endogenously to ensure non-negative wedges at the informality target.

\subsection{Reconciliation with higher substitution elasticities}\label{app:sigma_discussion}

Refugee-crisis studies report substitution elasticities between worker types of $\sigma\approx 11$--$30$ \citep{OttavianoPeri2012, BaharDiTellaGulek2025}. These magnitudes are not directly comparable to $\sigma_{\text{sub}}$ as defined here for three reasons. The margin differs. Refugee studies measure substitution between workers of different origin in a market-clearing equilibrium, while $\sigma_{\text{sub}}$ captures intra-firm substitution between formal and informal employment regimes. The horizon differs. Refugee studies identify medium-run responses that include firm entry and organizational reallocation, while the model here holds the firm distribution fixed. The unit differs. Refugee studies typically include self-employed workers, while the aggregator concerns salaried employment. DSGE models of informality \citep{LeyvaUrrutia2020, McKiernan2021} similarly use $\sigma$ parameters that govern sectoral mobility rather than intra-firm substitution. In \citet{Ulyssea2018}, formal and informal workers are effectively perfect substitutes within the firm, with productivity differences captured entirely by the distribution of firm types. The calibration $\sigma=1.326$ with $\eta_I=0.40$ can be read as a parametric decomposition in which the formal--informal wage ratio directly pins the per-efficiency-unit relative productivity of informal labor, and a modest intra-firm substitutability ($\sigma>1$) absorbs the remaining reallocation margin, both jointly disciplined by the observed wage premium.

\subsection{Disciplining $\sigma_{\text{sub}}$: full results}

For each $\sigma$ in a grid over $[0.40,3.00]$, the full model is recalibrated and the implied formal--informal wage premium is computed from the CES first-order condition,
\[
R(\sigma)=\frac{\omega}{1-\omega}\cdot\left(\frac{L_F}{L_I}\right)^{\rho-1}\cdot z,
\]
where $z=\text{eff}_{hF}/(\eta_I h_I e(h_I))$. The mapping is monotone in $\sigma$. At the recalibrated $(\omega,\eta_I)=(0.622,0.40)$, setting $R\in[1.15,1.55]$ delivers $\sigma\in[1.116,1.469]$ and the conservative midbox $A_{\text{req}}\in[7.50\%,8.58\%]$ (the full $(\sigma,\omega,\eta_I)$-box envelope is $[6.93\%,9.23\%]$). \citet{DerenoncourtGerardLagosMontialoux2025} estimate a small formal-to-informal reallocation elasticity of about $-0.28$ from the Brazilian minimum-wage passthrough, consistent with the modest intra-firm $\sigma$ used in this paper.

\section{Derivations}\label{app:derivations}

\subsection{$\kappa$ from $e_q$}\label{app:kappa_deriv}

With $e(h)=\exp\{-\kappa(h-h^*)^2\}$, the hours--output elasticity at $h_{\text{ref}}$ is
\[
e_q = \frac{d\ln(h\cdot e(h))}{d\ln h}\bigg|_{h=h_{\text{ref}}} = 1 - 2\kappa\,h_{\text{ref}}(h_{\text{ref}}-h^*),
\]
so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kappa_derivation}
\kappa=\frac{1-e_q}{2 h_{\text{ref}}(h_{\text{ref}}-h^*)}.
\end{equation}
With $e_q=0.60$, $h_{\text{ref}}=42.244$, and $h^*=40$, $\kappa=2.110\times 10^{-3}$, so $e(36)=e(44)=0.9668$ and $e(40)=1$.

\subsection{Firm first-order condition}

The firm problem has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:foc_NF}
\frac{\partial Y_g}{\partial L_{F,g}}\cdot h_{F,\text{avg}}\cdot e_F\cdot\omega\cdot\left(\frac{L_{F,g}}{L_g}\right)^{\rho-1}=\tau_g+\gamma_{F,g}(N_{F,g}-\bar{N}_{F,g})+\pi_m N_{I,g},
\end{equation}
where $\partial Y_g/\partial L_{F,g}=(1-\alpha)Y_g/L_g\cdot(\partial L_g/\partial L_{F,g})$ and $\partial L_g/\partial L_{F,g}=\omega(L_{F,g}/L_g)^{\rho-1}$.

\subsection{$A_{\text{req}}$ bisection monotonicity}

$A_{\text{req}}$ solves Equation~\eqref{eq:areq} of the main paper. The Cobb-Douglas structure $Y_g=A K_g^\alpha L_g^{1-\alpha}$ is linear in $A$ and the optimal $L_g(A)$ is weakly increasing in $A$, so $\partial\sum_g Y_g/\partial A>0$ and bisection converges to a unique $A_{\text{req}}$.
