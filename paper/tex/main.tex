% === Abstract ===
\begin{abstract}
Brazil's workweek debate began with a 36-hour proposal, but it now includes 40-hour and phased alternatives. This paper computes $A_{\text{req}}$, the one-off TFP gain that would make shorter formal hours output-neutral in a short-run accounting model with informality, firm-size heterogeneity, and an hours-productivity schedule. A 40-hour cap requires a modest productivity gain and leaves the representative agent GHH diagnostic positive. The 36-hour endpoint is more demanding, with an output-neutrality benchmark around $6$--$8\%$ depending on the hours-efficiency specification and disciplined calibration envelopes of roughly $5.6$--$7.5\%$ and $6.9$--$9.2\%$. The benchmark is large relative to Brazil's recent TFP record, while most of the output cost comes from fewer formal hours rather than from reallocation into informality. The results support evaluating intermediate and phased paths separately from a direct move to 36 hours; they do not rank reforms by social welfare.
\end{abstract}

\textbf{Keywords:} working hours, informality, total factor productivity, CES aggregator, Brazil, welfare
