The remaining parameters are set inside ranges consistent with evidence on work hours \citep{Pencavel2015,CollewetSauermann2017}, informal productivity \citep{LaPortaShleifer2014}, and payroll-compliance rules \citep{BrasilLei8212,ReceitaESocialRural2021}. Cross-country hours evidence in \citet{BickFuchsSchundelnLagakos2018} supports declining marginal product of hours, but it does not identify a peak. The online appendix reports the source and sensitivity for each parameter.

The CES substitution elasticity $\sigma_{\text{sub}}$ is disciplined by the wage premium rather than directly estimated. From the CES first-order condition, the formal-informal wage premium $R$ is a monotone function of $\sigma_{\text{sub}}$. Plugging a widened window around \citeauthor{MeghirNaritaRobin2015}'s estimates, $R\in[1.15,1.55]$, into the CES FOC at the recalibrated $(\omega,\eta_I)=(0.622,0.40)$ yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sigma_interval}
\begin{aligned}
R\in[1.15,1.55]&\implies \sigma_{\text{sub}}\in[1.116,1.469],\\
A_{\text{req}}&\in[7.50\%,8.58\%]\quad\text{(two-sided penalty, central box).}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The central value $\sigma=1.326$ corresponds to $R\approx 1.40$, the midpoint of the MNR window.\footnote{The window $R\in[1.15,1.55]$ spans the full range of MNR's reported estimates across sub-populations and alternative specifications (Tables~3 and~10). MNR's $R$ is a structural premium estimated inside their search-and-matching model. Inverting it through the CES first-order condition assumes that the relative wage MNR identifies between formal and informal workers coincides with the relative marginal product of formal vs.\ informal effective labor inside the CES aggregator. The online appendix reports a descriptive PNAD/SIDRA bridge using published monthly earnings by formal/informal category pair. The private-employee pair gives $R=1.24$, while the domestic-worker pair gives $R=1.58$. These are not controlled microdata regressions, but they show that current published Brazilian earnings gaps are close to the MNR structural window. The mapping from $R$ to $\sigma_{\text{sub}}$ depends on $(\omega,\eta_I)$. The anchors $\omega=0.622$ ($1$ minus the narrow PNAD 2025 informality rate) and $\eta_I=0.40$ (midpoint of the La Porta-Shleifer informal/formal wage ratio range $1/3$ to $1/2$) are theoretically consistent. Under competitive wage-taking, the CES aggregator identifies $\eta_I$ through the marginal-product (wage) ratio rather than through value-added per employee, which is contaminated by capital-per-worker differences. The online appendix documents the derivation.} The central value is an expositional reference point, not an identified elasticity. The main quantitative claims therefore report the joint $(\sigma,\omega,\eta_I)$ envelope alongside the central calibration.

The three wage-related objects play different roles. The formal weight $\omega$ fixes the level of formal effective labor using the narrow PNAD informality rate. The informal efficiency $\eta_I$ fixes the level penalty of informal effective labor, using the broad informal/formal wage-ratio literature. Conditional on those two level parameters, the wage premium $R$ identifies the curvature of formal-informal substitution, $\sigma_{\text{sub}}$, through the CES first-order condition. The joint $(\sigma,\omega,\eta_I)$ envelope below varies the three objects independently, so the headline interval does not rely on a single wage moment doing all the work.

This wage-premium-disciplined calibration supersedes earlier author-chosen values of $\sigma_{\text{sub}}$ used in preliminary rounds of the paper. It mainly changes incidence and the sign of the informality response. Under $\sigma_{\text{sub}}<1$, formal and informal labor are complements, so a tighter formal cap pushes inputs together and mutes the informality response. Under $\sigma_{\text{sub}}>1$, as disciplined here by the MNR window, they are imperfect substitutes, and the cap pushes marginal formal workers toward informality. For aggregate output, however, the dominant channel remains the mechanical contraction of formal hours: varying $\sigma$, $\omega$, and $\eta_I$ over the disciplined box moves the endpoint by about two percentage points, while the raw hours cut accounts for most of the loss. Letting $\omega$ and $\eta_I$ also vary over their data-consistent ranges ($\omega\in[0.58,0.66]$, $\eta_I\in[0.33,0.50]$), the joint $(\sigma,\omega,\eta_I)$-box envelope under the two-sided penalty specification is $[6.93\%, 9.23\%]$ at the eight corners. Under the one-sided fatigue specification the central $A_{\text{req}}$ is $6.63\%$ and the envelope is $[5.62\%,7.48\%]$. The online appendix reports the full grids. The small reallocation elasticity documented by \citet{DerenoncourtGerardLagosMontialoux2025} supports a modest intra-firm $\sigma$ and is distinct from the high substitution elasticities between worker types reported in refugee studies.

Rebuilding the calibration on PNAD habitual hours ($\theta=(0.143,0.406,0.451)$, $44.2\%$ informality) yields $A_{\text{req}}=7.80\%$ under the two-sided penalty specification. The baseline uses DIEESE contracted hours because the statutory cap is a contracted-hours concept.
